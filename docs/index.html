Built around how Arizona eats

Star ratings won't tell you which cart makes the Sonoran dog everyone lines up for, or which saloon on Whiskey Row still serves dinner. Arizona Eats is organized the way people here eat: Sonoran hot dogs, Sonoran Mexican, chimichangas, fry bread, Route 66 diners and frontier saloons, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Where a county publishes inspection ratings, you see the latest one in the county's own words, never a grade from us, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on a desert highway.

What stays on your phone

Arizona Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Arizona Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The Sonoran hot dog, Sonoran Mexican, chimichanga, fry bread, Route 66, saloon and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a 2025 or 2026 source behind each place: its website or menu, a dated local news story or an official tourism listing. The wider directory of 17,123 restaurants starts from Overture Maps' open place data, keeps the kinds of listing that proved reliable against Maricopa County's permit list, and adds restaurants from the county and city lists we can read.

Where can I get a Sonoran hot dog?

Our Sonoran hot dog list has 19 hand-checked stands and restaurants in 2 towns, most of them in Tucson. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location. Many are carts that open in the evening, so check before you drive.

How do I check an Arizona restaurant's inspection?

Arizona has no statewide grade: each county inspects its own restaurants. Mohave and Pinal counties publish their ratings as lists, and for places there the app and this site show the latest rating in the county's own words, with its date. Maricopa County shows a grade card on each restaurant's page on its own site, and the other counties have their own search pages.

What is the Golden Plate Award?

Yavapai County's yearly award for food establishments that went a whole calendar year without a cited priority food handling violation, with an approved food safety plan and a certified manager. We show it as the county's award, on the restaurants we could match from its list.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
